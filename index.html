What ambience is made of

Three signals but ultimately one impression.

Every space is already sending all three, chosen or not. We work on each one, at the level our licence and the evidence allow.

Scent

The fastest emotional route into a customer's head; smell is the only sense wired directly to the brain's memory and emotion centres, which is why a room can make someone feel something before they have decided anything. The right scent is chosen for your brand, your rooms and your clientele, then proven in the space.

Effect on customer expenditure. Most businesses sit between 0 and +3%. The −17% zone is real: the wrong scent, or the right scent done badly, actively pushes customers away. The +23% ceiling appears in service and wellness settings, which is the context we work in.

Fit matters as much as quality: in one clothing-store study, gender-congruent scents nearly doubled sales in each department, and swapping them reversed the effect.

Spangenberg et al., 2006. Journal of Business Research, 59(12)

Music

Music sets the pace of a room, and it either works with your scent or against it. When the two match in energy, customers rate the space higher, stay longer and buy more; when they clash, the room reads worse than silence. We audit what is playing and align it to the prescription.

Music and scent have to agree on energy

Congruent

Slow ambient with lavender. Fast pop with citrus. Christmas music with pine.

Incongruent

Spa music with bright citrus. Dance music with lavender. Christmas music with summer fruit.

Mattila and Wirtz, 2001. Journal of Retailing, 77(2) · Spangenberg et al., 2005. Journal of Business Research, 58(11)

When the arousal level of the music matches the scent, evaluations, impulse purchasing and satisfaction all rise. When they clash, the environment reads as less pleasing than doing nothing at all.

Climate

Temperature and air quality decide whether a room feels cared for, and they carry the scent itself. We measure both, trial freestanding air and diffusion units as part of the proof of concept, and refer anything inside walls or ductwork to licensed partners with an indicative cost.

Warmth carries a premium. Cold is the baseline.

Zwebner, Lee and Goldenberg, 2013. Journal of Consumer Psychology, 24(2)

In a controlled test, shoppers in a room set roughly 4°C above a comfortable 22°C were willing to pay 10.4% more across a basket of everyday products than shoppers in a room 4°C below it. Both rooms were rated equally pleasant, so this is not a comfort effect.

Room comparison drawn from 98 participants across 11 products; nine of the eleven moved upward. Treat as directional evidence, not a guaranteed return.

Why all three together

None of these signals works alone. Customers judge a space on the agreement between what they smell, hear and feel; get one wrong and it undoes the other two. That agreement is what we design.

Mattila and Wirtz, 2001, Journal of Retailing · Spence, Puccinelli, Grewal and Roggeveen, 2014, Psychology and Marketing · Spangenberg et al., 2006, Journal of Business Research
